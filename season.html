How the numbers work

Rest-of-season value (ROS): each player's projected fantasy points per game (preseason consensus, blended with this season's actual production, which counts more as games pile up) turned into an expected locked score per week, times the weeks he's expected to play from now to week 24. Known injuries (ESPN return dates, live Sleeper status) remove weeks.

This week: for each player, the expected locked score from what's left of the week. This includes the games he has left, the chance he plays them (Out 0%, Doubtful 25%, Questionable 65%, Day-to-day 80%), and a finished game you can still lock. The best lineup maximises the total.

Matchup: simulates the rest of your week thousands of times. Every starter on both teams plays out his remaining games (with injury odds) and gets locked or passed. It tests how aggressive your bars should be: when you're ahead, locking sooner protects the lead; when you're behind, holding out for big games gives you the upside you need. It uses whichever setting wins most often. For each game you can lock right now, it shows your win chance if you lock vs pass. Mark locks and mid-week pickups for both teams, since Sleeper doesn't publish them.

Lock-In: after a game, lock it if it beats the expected value of waiting for his remaining games. The bar rises with more games left and drops if he might miss games. If his next game has started, that game can no longer be locked. His last game of the week counts automatically.

Waivers: rank by rest of season (best long-term add and drop) or by this week / next 2 weeks for short-term pickups. Short-term mode uses Sleeper's per-game weekly projections, which react to role changes like a starter getting hurt. It only suggests dropping one of your 4 lowest rest-of-season players and shows what that drop costs over the season.

Weekly projections: Sleeper publishes a projection for every game this week and next. These set each player's level for this week's values and lock bars (80% weekly projection, 20% season level). They also feed the rest-of-season value, counting like 10 games of evidence alongside the preseason projection (15 games) and this season's actual games.

Betting lines: where Sleeper Picks has over/under lines for a game (usually the next day's games, about a dozen players each), the stats they cover are blended 70% lines / 30% Sleeper's projection, then scored with your league's settings. Those games show ($). Lines react faster to minutes and role changes.

Calibration: checked against every game of last season (fit on Oct–Jan, tested on Jan–Apr). Sleeper's rebounds are scaled by 0.87 and turnovers by 0.93 to remove their bias. A single game lands within about ±(4.3 + 0.14 × average) of its projection, so ±8.5 for a 30 FP/G player. With that spread, predicted locked scores matched what was actually locked within 1 point a week at every player level. Details in the README under Model checks.

Market value estimates how other managers see a player. It starts from his draft-day reputation (the projection of the player drafted where he was), then pulls toward this season's numbers quickly: 6 games count as much as the reputation. Our model is slower (15 games). A slumping player looks worse to his manager than to us, so he's a buy-low. A hot one looks better, so he's a sell-high.

Team needs: the share of a team's healthy starting lineup that's out injured, plus a losing record, gives an urgency from 0 to 100%. Urgent teams value production over the next ~3 weeks more, so they'll accept trades for healthy players they'd otherwise turn down.

Trades: both teams' lineup value before and after, using the same values. A 2-for-1 credits the open roster spot with a typical free agent. "Helps both" means our model says neither side loses value. Real managers may value players by name or ADP, so the market column helps you pitch it.
